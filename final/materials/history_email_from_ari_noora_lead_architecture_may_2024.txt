Noora (SevOne) components

 

In rough view, Noora consists of four parts:

Input
Custom - Integrations
Custom - Input data read
Custom - Provisioning Engine
Core
SevOne NMS - Device modelling
SevOne NMS - Metrics Polling
SevOne NMS - Data Categorization
SevOne NMS - Alarm management
Custom-UI
Custom - Realtime UIs
Custom - Categorization UIs
Custom - Management UIs
Output
Custom - Cron scheduling
Custom - Data export
 

Custom means that Tietoevry or Telia/Ari Lipponen has created content, from nothing. SevOne NMS means commercial product. In short, input reads data in and manages the SevOne product via custom build provisioning engine, which is basically a chain of workflows. Core is the SevOne NMS and it models devices, polls metrics, categorizes data and creates alarms. Custom-UI offers custom graphical user interfaces which some are integrated directly into network orchestrator. There are also UIs for categorization management and Noora system internal management. Output contains schedules and data export components.

 

The parts are created by:

Input                     : Tietoevry
Core                       : SevOne
Custom-UI          : Ari Lipponen
Output                 : Tietoevry

Amount of work in Noora

 

I will estimate this in a mount of events:

Critical issue, requiring SevOne (IBM) vendor support                      : once every two years (1 to 4 weeks)
Non-critical issue, requiring SevOne (IBM) vendor support             : once every two years (1 to 4 weeks)
Critical bug in the custom parts of the system                                      : once every year (1 to 3 days)
Non-critical bug issue in the custom parts of the system                 : once per six months (5-20 hours)
Issue with a provisioning, alarm or reporting (not a bug)                 : once per month (1-5 hours)
 

In general, the system has been stable from the start. I wrote critical issues once every two years, but even these are mostly related to SevOne software upgrades. You may need to understand that the flow from input to output, even extending from network orchestrator to CC fault ticketing and Qlik Sense on reporting side, is fully automated. We have no constant manual interactions in the middle. Therefore, as we don’t touch anything, we don’t either break anything. Usually, minor issues are seen when the networks are dramatically changed or totally new type of equipment are introduced or if changes are done to the OSS/BSS systems itself.
